Dr Mireia Crispin-Ortuzar is an Assistant Professor in the Department of Oncology at the University of Cambridge, where she leads the Crispin Lab at the Early Cancer Institute. She co-leads the Ovarian Programme at the CRUK Cambridge Centre and is part of the Mark Foundation Institute for Integrated Cancer Medicine.

Her research develops machine learning methods that integrate imaging, pathology, genomics and clinical data to predict how cancers will respond to treatment and to detect them earlier. Her work includes radiogenomic models of ovarian cancer response, multi-omic predictors of breast cancer therapy response, image-guided biopsy platforms and AI for kidney cancer detection.

She holds a DPhil in Particle Physics from the University of Oxford and worked at Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center, New York. She then moved to Cambridge as a postdoctoral researcher at the CRUK Cambridge Institute, before establishing her own group.
