Emma is a PhD researcher developing artificial intelligence to find and assess kidney cancer earlier. Kidney cancer is often found by chance, and many people who attend lung cancer screening have CT scans that capture part of the kidneys. This is a chance to spot kidney problems without an extra appointment. But reviewing these scans is time-consuming, and telling dangerous tumours apart from harmless cysts is difficult.

Emma's work builds on RADAR, the AI tool developed in the lab by William McGough to detect kidney cancer on CT scans taken without contrast dye. The project extends RADAR from detection to characterisation: combining CT images with clinical information, such as age, blood tests and previous scans, to estimate how aggressive a lesion is, and so help direct each patient to the right next step, whether that is prompt surgery, monitoring or no further action.

Along the way, Emma will test how well these tools cope with real screening scans, where the kidneys may be only partly in view, and validate the results on data from other hospitals.
